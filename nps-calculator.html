NPS Tier 1 vs Tier 2: Rules, Tax Benefits & PFRDA Guidelines

The National Pension System (NPS) is a government-sponsored, market-linked retirement scheme regulated by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA). It is widely recognized as one of India's most cost-effective and tax-efficient retirement vehicles, with fund management fees under 0.09% p.a.

🏆 Triple Tax Advantage of NPS (EEE / EET Status):

1. Section 80CCD(1): Up to 10% of salary (Basic + DA) within the ₹1.5 Lakh 80C umbrella.
2. Section 80CCD(1B): Exclusive additional deduction of ₹50,000 beyond 80C.
3. Section 80CCD(2): Employer contribution to NPS up to 10% (private sector) or 14% (government) of Basic + DA, exempt under both the Old and New Tax Regimes!
4. Section 10(12A): The 60% lump sum withdrawal at age 60 is 100% tax-free.

How the 60:40 Maturity Rule Operates

When an NPS subscriber reaches 60 years of age (superannuation):

  • 60% Lump Sum: You can withdraw up to 60% of the accumulated corpus directly to your bank account with zero income tax liability.
  • 40% Mandatory Annuity: PFRDA mandates that at least 40% of the corpus must be used to purchase an immediate annuity from an approved Annuity Service Provider (ASP) such as LIC of India, SBI Life, or HDFC Life, which pays you a guaranteed monthly pension for life.
  • Full Withdrawal Exemption: If your total accumulated corpus is ₹5 Lakh or less, you are entitled to withdraw 100% of the money without purchasing an annuity!

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How much pension will I get from a ₹1 Crore NPS corpus?
If you have ₹1 Crore at age 60: with a 40% annuity allocation (₹40 Lakhs) at a 6% annuity interest rate, your monthly pension will be ₹20,000/month for life, and you receive ₹60 Lakhs tax-free cash. If you allocate 100% of your corpus to annuity, your monthly pension would be ₹50,000/month.
Is the monthly NPS pension taxable?
While the 60% lump sum withdrawal is 100% tax-free, the monthly annuity pension received after retirement is considered taxable income in the year of receipt and taxed according to your applicable income tax slabs.
Can I invest in NPS if I choose the New Tax Regime?
Yes! While individual contributions under Section 80CCD(1B) are not deductible in the New Tax Regime, employer contributions to your NPS account under Section 80CCD(2) (up to 14% of Basic + DA for govt and 14% / 10% for corporate employees) remain fully deductible and tax-free even under the New Tax Regime.
