How the pay matrix increment works

~3% per cell

One cell to the right, every July

Each annual increment moves you exactly one cell forward in your pay level's row of the matrix — about 3% higher, rounded to the matrix's pre-computed figure.

DA & HRA scale with it

Your raise is bigger than just the basic increase

Since DA and HRA are both percentages of Basic Pay, your increment amount also pulls up your DA and HRA — the real monthly gain is more than the basic-pay jump alone.

NPS scales too

10% of the higher Basic+DA

Your NPS deduction is 10% of Basic+DA, so it also rises with your increment — this is subtracted from your gross gain to get your real net monthly increase.

DA ≥ 50% now

HRA at its highest slab: 30/20/10%

HRA steps up in stages as DA rises: 24/16/8% at launch, 27/18/9% once DA crossed 25%, and 30/20/10% once DA crossed 50% — the slab currently in force since DA is at 60%.

Commonly asked questions

How is annual increment calculated under 7th Pay Commission? +

Your annual increment moves you one cell to the right in the 7th CPC pay matrix for your pay level — an increase of approximately 3% over your current basic pay, using the matrix's pre-computed figures rather than a raw percentage calculation.

When is annual increment given to government employees? +

Annual increment is granted on 1st July each year, provided the employee has completed at least 6 months of service in their current pay cell by 30 June.

What is the current HRA rate for X, Y and Z class cities? +

HRA steps up as DA rises: 24% / 16% / 8% at baseline, 27% / 18% / 9% once DA crossed 25%, and 30% / 20% / 10% once DA crossed 50% — the slab currently in force for X-class (metro), Y-class, and Z-class cities respectively, since DA is now at 60%.

What happens when I reach the last cell of my pay level? +

If you reach the maximum cell of your pay level without a promotion, you continue receiving annual increments as stagnation increments for up to 2 more years, at the same rate as your last regular increment, until promotion or MACP upgradation.

Why does this tool only show 15 cells per level? +

Each pay level actually has up to 40 cells, but the first 15 cover most career stages relevant to a 1-3 year projection. If your current basic pay isn't listed for your level, you're likely deep into your pay band — check your payslip and contact your DDO/PAO for your exact next-cell figure.
