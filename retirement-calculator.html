What goes into your retirement package

NPS Corpus

Your contribution + employer's, compounded

10% of Basic+DA from you, 14% from the government, growing monthly at your expected return rate from your current NPS balance to retirement.

60/40 split

Lump sum vs lifetime annuity

At exit, 60% of your NPS corpus comes to you tax-free as a lump sum; the remaining 40% buys an annuity that pays you monthly for life.

Gratuity

Capped at ₹25 lakh

A one-time tax-free payout based on your last Basic+DA and years of service, capped at ₹25,00,000 regardless of the formula result.

Commutation

Trading future pension for cash now

You can commute up to 40% of your monthly pension for an upfront lump sum, using an age-based commutation factor — your monthly pension reduces accordingly.

Commonly asked questions

How accurate is this retirement projection? +

This tool computes every figure from the numbers you enter — your current NPS corpus, pay level, age, and service years — rather than using fixed demo values. That said, it still simplifies real-world variables like annual increment percentage, future DA revisions, and the exact six-monthly gratuity formula. Treat the output as a directional estimate, not a guaranteed figure.

Why do I need to enter my current NPS corpus? +

Your actual NPS balance depends on your full contribution history and past market returns, which this tool has no way of knowing. Entering your current balance from your CRA/NPS statement gives an accurate starting point for projecting growth to retirement.

What is pension commutation?

Commutation lets you convert part of your future monthly pension — up to 40% — into a lump sum paid immediately at retirement. Your monthly pension is reduced for a fixed period (commonly 15 years), after which it is typically restored to the full amount.

Should I rely on this for my actual retirement planning? +

Use this as a starting point to understand the moving parts of your retirement package. For exact figures used in your final settlement, confirm with your department's PAO/DDO or a financial advisor closer to your retirement date.
