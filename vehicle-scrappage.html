How India's scrappage policy works

15-yr rule

Private vehicles — 15 years, then fitness test

After 15 years, private vehicles must pass an RTO fitness test to renew registration, renewable every 5 years after that. In Delhi-NCR, petrol/CNG vehicles over 15 years are banned outright.

Delhi diesel: 10 yrs

Delhi-NCR diesel vehicles get only 10 years

Under NGT orders, diesel vehicles in Delhi-NCR are banned after 10 years — no fitness renewal option. Only option is scrappage or transferring to another state with an NOC.

Commercial: 15 yrs

Commercial vehicles — 15 years nationally

All commercial vehicles (trucks, buses) must pass a mandatory fitness test every 2 years after the first 2 years of registration, and are typically retired after 15 years.

Green Tax

Extra levy at each fitness renewal

Vehicles older than 8 years are charged green tax — roughly 10–25% of road tax — at each fitness renewal, as a financial nudge toward scrapping older, more polluting vehicles.

Commonly asked questions

What is the vehicle scrappage policy in India? +

India's National Vehicle Scrappage Policy requires all private vehicles older than 20 years and commercial vehicles older than 15 years to undergo a fitness test or be scrapped. In Delhi-NCR under NGT orders, petrol vehicles over 15 years and diesel vehicles over 10 years are banned from operating.

What is the green tax on old vehicles in India? +

Green Tax is levied on older vehicles at the time of fitness renewal. Private vehicles older than 8 years are charged green tax at 10% to 25% of road tax at each fitness renewal, with the rate varying by state and vehicle age.

What happens when a vehicle completes 15 years in India? +

When a private vehicle completes 15 years, it must pass an RTO fitness test to renew its registration. The registration is renewed for 5 years at a time after that, along with payment of green tax. In Delhi-NCR, petrol vehicles over 15 years are banned from roads entirely.

What is the scrapping value of a car in India? +

Scrap value depends on vehicle weight, metal type, and current scrap rates. A typical car fetches between ₹20,000 and ₹50,000 at a registered scrapping centre, while a two-wheeler typically gets ₹3,000 to ₹6,000. Government scrapping centres also offer a waiver on road tax for the new vehicle purchase.
