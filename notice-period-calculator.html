Notice Period Buyout in India: Rules, Calculation & Legal Rights

In the Indian corporate and IT ecosystem, notice periods typically range from 30 days to 90 days (3 months). When an employee receives an urgent offer from a new organisation or needs an early release due to personal exigencies, a Notice Period Buyout allows the employee (or their new employer) to pay the monetary equivalent of the unserved notice days in exchange for immediate relieving.

⚠️ Crucial Circular on GST (Ministry of Finance, India):

According to CBIC Circular No. 178/10/2022-GST dated 3rd August 2022, notice pay recovery collected by an employer from an employee for leaving before the agreed notice period is not taxable under GST. Employers cannot legally demand 18% GST on notice shortfall recovery. If your HR adds GST to your Full & Final (F&F) settlement, quote this circular to get it removed.

How is the Per-Day Salary Calculated for Buyout?

The per-day rate depends on two primary factors specified in your employment contract:

  • Salary Base (Basic vs Gross): In over 90% of established Indian IT companies (including TCS, Infosys, Wipro, and Cognizant), notice recovery is calculated on Monthly Basic Salary only. However, some startups and mid-size firms define notice recovery on Gross Salary or Fixed CTC.
  • Divisor (30 Days vs 26 Working Days): Most companies use a standard 30-day calendar divisor (Basic / 30). Certain manufacturing and industrial companies under the Factories Act or state Shops and Establishment Acts use a 26 working days divisor (Basic / 26).

Can Earned Leave (EL/PL) be Adjusted Against Notice Period?

Yes, subject to employer discretion and company leave policy. In Indian labor practice, accumulated Privilege Leaves (PL) or Earned Leaves (EL) can either be:

  • Encashed during F&F: Paid to you at retirement or resignation under Section 10(10AA).
  • Offset against Notice Shortfall: Subtracted directly from the number of days you need to serve or buy out, significantly reducing your out-of-pocket settlement amount.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How is notice period buyout calculated in India?
The standard formula is: (Basic Salary ÷ 30) × (Shortfall Notice Days − Leave Offset). For instance, if your basic salary is ₹60,000, your daily rate is ₹2,000. If you serve 30 days out of a 90-day notice period and adjust 10 earned leaves, your net shortfall is 50 days, making the buyout amount ₹1,00,000.
Can my company refuse to allow a notice period buyout?
Yes. Notice period buyout is an option, not an automatic statutory right. If your project deliverables are critical, or if a replacement has not been hired for client handover, the company has the contractual right to require you to serve the full notice period.
Will I get my Relieving Letter and Experience Certificate if I buy out?
Yes! Once the notice buyout is mutually agreed upon, formalised, and settled in the Full & Final (F&F) statement, the employer must release your Relieving Letter, Service Certificate, and Form 16. The relieving letter simply mentions your final working day.
Is notice buyout reimbursed by a new employer taxable?
Yes. Under Section 17(2) of the Income Tax Act, any monetary reimbursement given by a new employer to pay off your previous company is classified as a perquisite or taxable allowance. Your new employer will withhold TDS according to your tax slab.
